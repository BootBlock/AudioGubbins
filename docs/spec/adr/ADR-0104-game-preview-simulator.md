# ADR-0104 — The Game Preview Is A Seeded Simulation Of Authored Rules, Played As A Generated Plan, And Its Algorithms Are The Reference The Godot Runtime Reproduces

- **Status:** Proposed
- **Decision:** `REQ-GAME-122`'s preview is a deterministic simulation in `packages/game-audio/src/preview/` whose result is played by the existing engine as a plan. It never changes the project.
  - **Rules and scenarios.** The rules are the `playback` policy field (`ADR-0101`): the selection rule, pitch and gain ranges, the start-offset range, the voice limit and stealing policy, the cooldown, and the attenuation. A `GamePreviewScenario` is the test bench: the space (2D or 3D), the listener, up to sixteen emitters, each with a subject (a group, whose members are its pool, or one member), a trigger pattern (by hand, or every interval with jitter for a count) and a motion (still, or along timed waypoints), and a seed. Scenarios are project objects; what the person drags or types while listening is the session's until they keep it.
  - **One generator.** Every random choice is a draw of the counter-based generator the dither uses (`lowbias32` over a key, a stream and an index, a multiple of 2⁻³²), moved to `packages/domain` so there is one: the key is the scenario's seed mixed with the emitter's place, the stream names the decision (selection, pitch, gain, offset, jitter), and the index is the trigger's ordinal. A decision therefore does not depend on what was drawn before, a trigger the cooldown suppresses changes no later choice, and the same scenario and triggers give the same choices.
  - **Selection.** `uniform` takes member ⌊u·n⌋ of the pool in its order; `avoid-recent` with a depth d from 1 to 16 removes the last min(d, n − 1) chosen and takes ⌊u·m⌋ of the m left in pool order. These are the strategies the preview needs; weighted, sequential, ping-pong and shuffle-bag strategies and custom hooks are Phase 13's (`REQ-GAME-113`), added to the same rule union over the same generator.
  - **Voices.** Each emitter plays at most its limit of voices (1 to 64), as a game engine's player does. A trigger within the cooldown of the last accepted one is suppressed; one past the limit steals the oldest voice, or the quietest (ties to the oldest), or is rejected, as the rule says; a stolen or stopped voice fades out over 5 ms. A member with a loop plays to its loop's end and cycles until it is stopped or stolen; a one-shot plays to its end. The simulation writes an event log: each trigger and voice, started, suppressed, rejected, stolen, stopped or ended, at a frame, with the member, pitch in cents, gain in dB and offset, which is what tests pin and the panel lists.
  - **Space.** Distance attenuation is a curve of the rule: none; inverse, inverse square or logarithmic of distance over a unit size, capped at a ceiling and silent past a maximum distance; or `(1 − d / max)` to an exponent; with an optional low-pass whose cutoff falls to a stated frequency at the maximum distance. Panning is equal-power from the emitter's azimuth to the listener, through a new canonical `pan` processor. A bus is a gain per bus name, muted or soloed by the person. The curves are named for and written to match the attenuation models Godot 4 documents; parity is Phase 11's to prove against them.
  - **Played as a plan.** The event log over a horizon becomes an `EditPlan`: each pitched voice a varispeed stream, a new kind of the domain's stream whose frames are its source's declared at another rate, so the canonical resampler changes speed and pitch together as an engine's pitch scale does; voices laid in lanes, one per voice slot, of silence and voice segments with their gain and fade stages; each emitter a mix of its lanes through a chain of gain, `pan` and filter; each bus a mix through a gain. Moving an emitter, the listener or a bus fader is a running parameter change of those chains, heard at once. The programme is extended without a break by a *continued* programme: a new plan identical up to a frame at or after the one the feeder reads next replaces the old from that frame, so a trigger by hand is heard within the feed-ahead time and a cycling loop never ends. The preview reuses the engine through `PlaybackControl` and makes no second playback system.
  - **The reference for the Godot runtime.** The generator, the selection rules, the voice and cooldown rules and the attenuation curves are specified exactly and pinned by test vectors (seed, rules, triggers to event log) in `packages/game-audio`. Phase 11's runtime addon, which does not yet exist, reproduces them and is held to the same vectors in GUT, which is how "equivalent runtime-selection logic" is met when the authoring tool comes first (`REQ-GAME-122`, `REQ-GAME-113`'s deterministic seeded selection).
  - **Not the runtime.** The preview is audition. It runs no project command, writes no history and holds its programme as audio runtime state; event parameters are the built-in ones (distance and position, volume and pitch offsets, bus levels); authored runtime parameters with curves, layered events and conditions are Phase 11's (`REQ-GODOT-114`, `REQ-GODOT-128`, `REQ-GODOT-129`).
- **Drivers:** `REQ-GAME-122` (rapid triggering, variation selection, pitch and gain randomisation, repetition avoidance, concurrency, stealing, cooldowns, 2D and 3D attenuation, distance, panning and movement, buses, seeded determinism, event parameters, the same rules as the runtime), the packet's invariant that preview is audition and its acceptance criterion of repeated one-shot and loop scenarios without changing authoritative edits, `ADR-0051` (the plan is what the engine plays), `ADR-0032` (real-time playback is not canonical, offline renders are). The engine has no triggered voices, no loop and no varispeed; a voice bank beside the plan would be a second description of what is heard.
- **Constraints:** the simulation is pure and bounded: at most 64 voices an emitter, sixteen emitters, a 30-second horizon a plan, 50 triggers a second. A continued programme is refused where the feeder has already read past its frame, and the preview tries again later. The `pan` processor is canonical, with its golden test. A varispeed stream is never persisted: an insertion's payload holding one is refused.
- **Change record:** affected requirements `REQ-GAME-122`, `REQ-GAME-113`, `REQ-GAME-023` (controlled random variation, as far as the preview), `REQ-ARCH-049`; affected phases 10, 11 (whose runtime reproduces these algorithms and maps these rules), 13 (whose selection strategies and variation sets extend them) and 03 (whose engine gains the continued programme). Compatibility impact: scenarios in the project document (`ADR-0106`). Public API: `GamePreviewScenario`, `PlaybackRules`, `SelectionRule`, `Attenuation`, `simulatePreview`, `PreviewEventLog`, `previewPlan` in `@audiogubbins/game-audio`; the counter draw in `@audiogubbins/domain`; the varispeed stream in the plan; the `pan` processor type; the continued programme of `@audiogubbins/audio-runtime`. Godot interchange and runtime: the test vectors. PWA and browser: none. Verification: determinism and rule properties over generated scenarios; the vectors; the plan rendered offline against the log; a browser test triggering a scenario repeatedly with the project's history unchanged.
- **Amends:** `ADR-0051`, whose plan gains the varispeed stream; `ADR-0030`, whose transport gains the continued programme. Their other clauses stand.
- **Related requirements:** `REQ-GAME-122`, `REQ-GAME-112`, `REQ-GAME-113`, `REQ-GODOT-114`, `REQ-GODOT-128`, `REQ-ARCH-049`, `REQ-ARCH-081`, `REQ-ARCH-153`.
